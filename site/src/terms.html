For the AgentAction mobile app, this website and the hosted approval service. Last updated 25 September 2026.

AgentAction lets a person approve or refuse things an AI assistant wants to do. These terms cover the app you install and the hosted service. The source code is separate, and is covered by its own licence.

The three things you might be using

  • The app. What you install on a phone to approve or refuse requests. Free to use.

  • The source code. Published at github.com/avijeett007/agentaction under the Apache 2.0 licence. That licence governs the code, and nothing on this page narrows it. You may run it yourself.

If you are the person approving

You need no account and you agree to nothing beyond this page. You pair your phone with whoever set up the assistant, and you can unpair at any time from Accounts.

A decision is yours. When you approve something, the action goes ahead. Read what you are approving — the four-digit code on screen should match the one you were told, and if it does not, refuse it.

If you do nothing, the request expires and the action does not happen. Silence is a refusal, never an approval.

If you are connecting an assistant

You are responsible for which actions you put behind approval and which you do not. AgentAction enforces the rules you set; it does not decide them for you, and it cannot know that an action you left ungated should have been gated.

You are responsible for the text your assistant puts in a request. The person approving sees it and relies on it. Do not put anything misleading in it, and do not put credentials or personal data in it.

You must have the right to ask the people you pair with to approve things, and you are responsible for telling them what you do with their decisions.

What we do not promise

Said plainly, because a security product that overstates itself is worse than one that does not exist:

  • AgentAction does not make an AI assistant safe. It puts a person in front of the actions you chose. An action you did not gate runs without asking.
  • It cannot tell whether an action is a good idea. That is the judgement of the person approving.
  • Notifications depend on Apple's and Google's networks and on the phone being online. They can be late or lost. The app also checks for itself, but a request can still go unseen and expire.
